`46c7a0d` (merged in; it includes the pilot visor and the ground-unit

## What was wrong (before)

| Area | Before |
|---|---|
| Occlusion | None baked. Intake, pylon roots, wing roots, gear doors and flap gaps were as bright as open skin under the sun; screen-space GTAO only touches indirect light and only at screen scale. |
| Relief | The normal maps were almost flat (the 1024 px airframe normal map compressed to 9.9 kB). Panel lines and rivets were paint only. |
| Resolution | 1024 px for the whole airframe: about 1.7 cm per texel, 1:1 with the screen at 17 m and magnified inside that. A shader unsharp mask stood in for detail. |
| Material | No roughness or metalness map. Roughness was guessed in the shader from albedo contrast; paint had metalness 0.18 and a clearcoat everywhere, including over soot and bare metal. The source's `KHR_materials_specular` map (a bare-metal mask, black elsewhere) tinted the paint's own specular to nothing. |
| Bare metal | Nozzle and jet pipe were dielectric dark grey. |
| Weathering | Only what the source albedo carried. No gun soot, no streaks tied to panels, no walkway wear, no leading-edge erosion. |
| Cockpit | One constant occlusion (0.42) for the whole tub: the panel face, the footwell and the sills all received the same share of sky. |

## What changed

Everything is generated by `tools/blender/build-mig-skin.mjs` (Node,
`gltf-transform` + `sharp`, with its own BVH ray tracer in
`mig-skin-trace.mjs`). No GLB was edited by hand and Blender is not needed.

1. **Baked ambient occlusion.** Every texel of every skin and cockpit atlas
   is rasterised to its model-space position and normal, then 96
   cosine-weighted rays are traced against all opaque triangles of the jet
   (3 m reach with distance falloff, control surfaces at neutral, glass and
   decal cards transparent). Texels whose normal points into the body are
   traced from the other side (the model is drawn two-sided). Baked once on
   `mig21.glb` at up to 1024 px and reused by the other files, which share
   its UV layout. It goes to the material's `aoNode` (indirect light).
   - Because AO cannot shade direct sun, the deepest cavities also lose up to
     34% of albedo ("dirt held in cavities").
   - The jet-pipe interior is floored at 0.5: bare metal walls light each
     other, and the geometric value left a black disc in the chase view.
   - All skin AO is floored at 0.14.
2. **Panel lines and rivets in the normal map.** A black top-hat filter on
   the albedo keeps dark marks narrower than a few texels (lines, rivets,
   latches) and rejects the edges of roundels, stains and colour changes.
   The mask becomes a height field (grooves and dimples) whose gradient is
   added to the source normal map, so relief and painted line register
   exactly.
3. **2048 px player airframe** (base colour and normal). The panel lines are
   redrawn from the line mask, so they stay thin when magnified. The shader
   unsharp mask is no longer applied to that atlas.
4. **Weathering, painted in model space** (continuous over UV seams and
   across materials):
   - panel-to-panel tone and gloss (flood fill between lines, +-3.6% tone),
     plus low-frequency repaint patches;
   - exhaust soot over the last metre of the fuselage and scorched paint
     ahead of it;
   - gun soot aft of the GSh-23 muzzles on the belly pack;
   - 215 oil and hydraulic streaks that start on strong panel-line texels of
     the sides and belly and run aft with a little sag (30% are still wet:
     glossier than the paint);
   - walkway scuffs on the upper wing roots;
   - leading-edge erosion on everything that faces the airflow: a dull
     sand-blasted band and sparse chips to primer and metal;
   - belly grime, heaviest on the centre line and aft of the wheel wells.
5. **Material response: one ORM texture per material** (occlusion, roughness,
   metalness), in place of the specular mask.
   - Paint: dielectric, base roughness 0.43 (hostile 0.47), +-0.05 per panel,
     smoother on newer paint and markings (0.37), rougher in soot (0.82),
     grime, scuffs and eroded edges, 0.2 in wet streaks.
   - Bare metal (from the source mask): metalness 0.92, brushed roughness
     0.30 to 0.46, base colour lifted to a metal F0, temper colours (straw,
     bronze, violet, blue-grey) along the jet pipe.
   - Chipped edges: metalness up to 0.9.
   - No clearcoat on the skin. The player skin is now a standard material,
     not a physical one.
6. **Cockpit.** The interior and the seat get the baked AO (mean 0.23 over the
   tub; panel face and footwell lowest, sills highest) instead of the
   constant, with a gain of 1.3 for light bounced inside the tub, and up to
   45% albedo loss in fully enclosed texels as a stand-in for the sun
   shadowing the shadow map cannot resolve. Their base colour and normal
   bytes are untouched.
7. **Hostiles and LODs.** `mig21_hostile.glb`, `mig21_hostile_lod1.glb` and
   `mig21-low.glb` go through the same script with the same AO and the same
   streak seeds, at their existing texture sizes. Hostiles stay unmarked;
   the script adds no markings anywhere. Chips are 30% weaker on the dark
   hostile paint.
8. **Runtime** (`src/assets/materials.js`): one fetch of the ORM texture
   feeds `aoNode`, `roughnessNode` and `metalnessNode`.

**Canopy and glass:** checked, not changed. In `canopy-close` the glass
shows the sky reflection and the sun glint, the frame and sill are sealed
(no light leak, the sill is now occluded under the rail), and the pilot's
tinted visor draws correctly through the canopy after the merge.

## Texture memory and download size

GPU memory is RGBA8 with mips, summed over each file's textures
(`node tools/blender/inspect-glb.mjs <file>`).

| File | Textures | GPU before (MiB) | GPU after (MiB) | Bytes before | Bytes after |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `mig21.glb` | 33 → 33 | 103.0 | 135.0 (1.31x) | 4,650,236 | 6,160,124 |
| `mig21_hostile.glb` | 33 → 33 | 87.0 | 87.0 | 4,391,356 | 5,359,532 |
| `mig21_hostile_lod1.glb` | 33 → 33 | 33.0 | 33.0 | 1,053,184 | 1,396,052 |
| `mig21_cockpit.glb` | 2 | 2.7 | 2.7 | 46,320 | 46,320 |
| **Loaded by the game** | | **225.7** | **257.7 (1.14x)** | 10,141,096 | 12,962,028 |
| `mig21-low.glb` (not loaded by any code) | 33 → 33 | 33.0 | 33.0 | 4,745,152 | 5,113,616 |

- The budget was 2x the MiG texture memory; the result is 1.14x overall and
  1.31x for the player. The only growth is the 2048 px player airframe base
  colour and normal map (+32 MiB). The ORM maps took the slots and sizes of
  the specular masks.
- Download grows by 2.82 MB (2.69 MiB): the ORM and normal maps carry real
  detail where the old maps were nearly empty. Textures are WebP, as before
  (base q88, normal q92, ORM q90 with sharp chroma subsampling).

## GPU cost

Whole-frame GPU timestamp (`renderer.info.render.timestamp`, `?gputime`),
1600 x 900, tier `high`, A/B inside one page: the pre-pass `mig21.glb` was
loaded as a second airframe through the old material path and the two were
swapped by visibility, three rounds of 14 s each per state
(`../polish-realism/gpucost.js`). The GPU was shared with other agents'
pages (this page ran at about 19 fps), the timer is quantised to 0.065 ms and
resolved only 7 to 9 times per 14 s, so these are coarse.

| Pose | Before (ms) | After (ms) | Delta | Per-round deltas |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `mig-skin-close` (skin fills the frame) | 0.983 | 0.983 | 0 | -0.13, 0, +0.07 |
| `mig-side-noon` | 0.983 | 0.983 | 0 | -0.13, -0.07, +0.07 |
| `cockpit` | 0.983 | 1.049 | +0.066 | 0, +0.07, +0.07 |

Within the +0.1 ms budget; the cockpit figure is one timer quantum. The
shader does less than before on the skin: one ORM fetch replaces two albedo
fetches for the roughness guess and the specular-map fetches, the 2048 px
atlas drops the unsharp fetch, and the clearcoat lobe is gone. Not measured:
2560 x 1440, other tiers, an idle GPU.

## Re-running

```
node tools/blender/build-mig-skin.mjs          # all four files, about 4 min the first time (AO bake), 45 s after
node tools/blender/build-mig-skin.mjs --debug  # also writes the intermediate maps to node_modules/.cache/mig-skin/debug
node tools/blender/build-mig-skin.mjs --rebake # throw the cached AO away
node tools/blender/write-catalog.mjs
```

The inputs are the GLBs at commit `852e0dc` (read from git into
`node_modules/.cache/mig-skin/in`), so the script always starts from the
same files. To shoot: load `../polish-realism/bench.js`, then `poses.js`,
then `await __bench.hostiles(); await __bench.pose('mig-skin-close')`.

## Left open

- **Sun shadowing in the cockpit is still approximated.** The baked AO puts
  the panel in a recess for indirect light and the albedo term stands in
  for direct light, but a real coaming shadow that moves with the sun needs
  a dedicated high-resolution shadow cascade for the cockpit or a baked
  bent-normal / visibility cone. The forward view changes only modestly.
- **The canopy arch seen from the seat** is low-poly and blotchy (it was
  before as well); the AO follows the facets. It needs geometry, not maps.
- **Derived relief is only as good as the source albedo.** Stencil text is
  engraved slightly, and the 512 px LOD atlases hold little line detail.
- **Hostile LOD0 keeps its 1024 px airframe**, so a hostile seen from 10 m
  is softer than the player.
- **AO is baked with stores fitted and surfaces neutral.** The pylons keep
  their contact shadows after a missile leaves.
- **Heat tint and soot are static.** They do not respond to afterburner use.
- **Sharpening halo:** the upscaled airframe shows a faint light rim around
  some access panels on the wing. It reads as worn panel edges, but it is an
  artefact of the unsharp step.
- `mig21-low.glb` is not referenced by any code. It was given the same
  treatment as asked, at 0.37 MB extra in the repository; it could be
  deleted instead.
- `tools/blender/build-mig-player.mjs` / `build-mig-variants.mjs` rebuild
  from the Sketchfab source and would drop this pass (and the earlier
  in-place passes). `BASE` in the script would have to move with them.
