# Art assets: evidence and notes

All models: metres, +Y up, front toward -Z, meshopt + quantized, WebP textures.
Load them with `loadModel(id, {lod, burned})` from `src/assets/loadModel.js`;
ids, sockets, pivots, triangle counts and sizes are in `src/assets/catalog.js`
(generated by `node tools/blender/write-catalog.mjs`). Renders are JPEG copies of
the PNGs the scripts write.

## Cockpit feasibility: verdict

**The existing `mig21.glb` already has a usable cockpit.** The War Thunder-derived
model has a full MiG-21 Bison interior (~30k tris): textured instrument panel with
legible round gauges and the MFD bezel, the gunsight/HUD combiner glass, the
windscreen arch, canopy glass, side consoles, stick, throttle and the ejection seat
with its headrest. It is not a shell, so nothing needs to be rebuilt.

| view | file |
| --- | --- |
| forward from the measured eye | `cockpit/eye_forward.jpg` |
| down at the panel | `cockpit/eye_down_panel.jpg` |
| left / right / back over the shoulder | `cockpit/eye_left.jpg`, `cockpit/eye_right.jpg`, `cockpit/eye_back_left.jpg` |
| centreline section with both eye points | `cockpit/side_section.jpg`, `cockpit/side_eye_markers.jpg` |
| the current Aircraft.js socket | `cockpit/socket_forward.jpg` (it sits outside, above the canopy) |
| in-game WebGPU, with the overlay, near = 0.03 | `cockpit/ingame_cockpit_eye.jpg` |

What it means for integration:

1. **The `cockpit` socket in `src/flight/Aircraft.js` (0, 0.95, -3.1) is outside the
   aircraft.** It is 0.5 m above the canopy top. The bbox centre is not the fuselage
   centreline, because the fin and the ventral tank stretch the box. Measured in the
   normalised model space: canopy apex y = 0.43 at z = -2.75, seat headrest at z ≈ -2.75,
   instrument panel at z ≈ -3.4. **Pilot eye = (0, 0.22, -2.95)**, and the gunsight
   combiner centre = (0, 0.212, -3.364). Both are in the catalog (`mig21.socketPositions.eye`
   and `.hud`) and in `mig21_cockpit.glb`. Aircraft.js is lead-owned, so I did not edit it.
   The change is one line: `this.sockets.cockpit.position.set(0, 0.22, -2.95)`.
2. **The camera near plane must drop to about 0.03–0.05 m in cockpit view.** The global
   0.5 m clips the panel (0.45 m from the eye) and the canopy arch. Reversed depth makes
   that safe.
3. The interior textures are 1024 px, so they are soft at 1440p. The original Sketchfab
   source is no longer in the repo; `tools/optimize-model.mjs` capped it at 1024. Re-running
   it with a 2048 cap on only the cockpit texture (`Image_2`, mesh `Object_9`) would sharpen
   the gauges.

**`mig21_cockpit.glb` (132 tris)** is an overlay, not a replacement. Parent it to
`aircraft.model` with an identity transform. It adds:
- emissive display faces, ray-cast onto the real panel: the radar MFD and four small
  displays;
- a canopy periscope;
- socket nodes `eye` and `hud`.

Check renders: `cockpit/overlay_eye_*.jpg`. Built by `tools/blender/build_cockpit.py`.

## Hostile MiG-21

- `mig21_hostile.glb`: 234k tris, 5.2 MB. Same airframe in a fictional two-tone
  grey-blue scheme with light-grey undersides.
  - The camo is 3D noise mirrored across the centreline. Each texel's 3D position is
    found by rasterising the triangles in UV space, so the pattern does not break at
    UV seams.
  - Roundels and fin flashes are found by colour (orange components plus nearby or
    enclosed green) and painted out. The green dielectric panels are repainted dark grey.
  - `mig21_hostile_airframe_texture.jpg` is the repainted main texture.
- `mig21_hostile_lod1.glb`: 24.2k tris, 1.1 MB, 512 px. Built by
  `tools/blender/build_mig_lod1.py`, which welds panel seams by position, drops parts
  under 6 cm and does a weighted collapse.
  - Known artefact: small notches around the intake lip. They are not visible at the
    600 m switch distance.
  - `mig21_hostile_lod0_vs_lod1.jpg`: LOD1 on the right.
- Both are **baked to the runtime conventions** (centred, nose -Z, 15.76 m). Load them
  with `loadModel`, not `new Aircraft({url})`: Aircraft.js would rotate them a further
  180°.
- Licence: CC BY 4.0 derivative. Attribution is in `public/THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.txt` and
  in the GLB `asset.extras`.

## Control surfaces: shipped (see `docs/v2/evidence/polish-art-hud/`)

Update: stabilators, ailerons and flaps are now cut by
`tools/blender/build_mig_surfaces.py` and ship as pivoted nodes in `mig21.glb` and
`mig21_hostile.glb` (both LODs). `Object_12` turned out to be the pylons, not the flaps;
the flaps are cut from the airframe like the ailerons. The rudder and airbrakes are
still fused (below). The original survey follows.

`tools/blender/probe_surfaces.py` lists the large loose parts of the joined airframe
mesh (`Object_13`):

| surface | found as | separable? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| stabilators | 2 islands per side, x ±0.48..1.95, z -7.47..-4.47 (three), 834 faces | yes, cleanly; hinge is the root spar near z ≈ -5.6 |
| ailerons | about 4 islands per side at the outer trailing edge, x ±2.04..3.26, z 2.64..3.21 | yes, with care (upper and lower skin plus end caps) |
| flaps | (`Object_12` is the pylons) cut from the airframe at the inboard trailing edge | yes |
| rudder | fused into the two rear-fuselage/fin islands (1288 faces each) | no: the fin geometry would have to be re-cut |
| airbrakes | flush ventral panels, not split from the skin | no |

(Splitting them meant a baked player GLB; Aircraft.js now skips normalising baked roots.)

## Weapons and ground units

These are procedural, built by `tools/blender/build_assets.py` (`kit.py`,
`assets_weapons.py`, `assets_ground.py`).
- Materials are procedural paint with dust collecting low on each object, then baked in
  Cycles to one atlas per asset: albedo with about 55% of the AO multiplied in, and an
  ORM map (glTF occlusion, roughness and metalness).
- Destroyed variants are rebuilt with damage (tilted or missing parts, debris) and a
  soot/rust palette.
- For anything without a `*_destroyed` GLB, `loadModel(id, {burned: true})` applies the
  charred material recipe.

**Budgets** (LOD0 triangles; enforced in `src/assets/catalog.test.mjs`):

Pivot convention:
- `turret`, `dish` and `rotor` turn on `rotation.y`.
- `barrels`, `barrel` and `launcher` turn on `rotation.x`; positive elevates.
- `tail_rotor` turns on `rotation.x`.
- Sockets are children of the moving part, so `getWorldPosition` follows aim.

## Rebuild

```
node tools/blender/decode-glb.mjs public/models/mig21.glb assets/authoring/build/mig21_decoded.glb
blender -b --factory-startup --python tools/blender/inspect_mig.py      # normalised .blend
blender -b --python tools/blender/render_cockpit.py                     # cockpit evidence
blender -b --python tools/blender/build_cockpit.py
node tools/blender/optimize-glb.mjs assets/authoring/build/mig21_cockpit_raw.glb public/models/mig21_cockpit.glb --tex 256
node tools/blender/build-mig-variants.mjs                               # hostile LOD0 (+ raw copy)
blender -b --python tools/blender/build_mig_lod1.py
node tools/blender/optimize-glb.mjs assets/authoring/build/mig21_hostile_lod1_raw.glb public/models/mig21_hostile_lod1.glb --tex 512
blender -b --factory-startup --python tools/blender/build_assets.py [-- ids]
node tools/blender/write-catalog.mjs
blender -b --python tools/blender/render_thumbs.py -- assets/authoring/build/thumbs public/models/<id>.glb ...
node tools/blender/contact-sheet.mjs assets/authoring/build/thumbs docs/v2/evidence/assets/contact_sheet.png 6
```

The `blender` above is `"C:/Program Files/Blender Foundation/Blender 5.2/blender.exe"`.
Intermediate files go to `assets/authoring/build/`, which is git-ignored.
